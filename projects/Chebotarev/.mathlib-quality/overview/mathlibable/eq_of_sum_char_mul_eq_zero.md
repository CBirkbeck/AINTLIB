### Statement (Phase 1)

`eq_of_sum_char_mul_eq_zero` states: **vanishing nontrivial Fourier coefficients force a constant.**
Let `G` be a finite abelian group and `f : G → ℂ`. If for every nontrivial complex character
`χ : G →* ℂˣ` the character moment `∑_{s∈G} χ(s)·f(s) = 0`, then `f` is constant — `f(u) = f(u')`
for all `u, u' ∈ G`.

This is the function-level conclusion of **finite-abelian Fourier inversion**: a function whose
Fourier transform is supported only on the trivial character is constant (its only surviving
Fourier component is the mean).

Variables / typeclasses (Lean side):
- `{G : Type*}`, `[CommGroup G]`, `[Fintype G]` — a finite abelian group (multiplicative notation).
- `(f : G → ℂ)` — a complex-valued function on `G`.
- `(u u' : G)` — the two evaluation points.

Hypotheses (Lean side):
- `hf : ∀ χ : G →* ℂˣ, χ ≠ 1 → ∑ s : G, ((χ s : ℂˣ) : ℂ) * f s = 0` — every **nontrivial**
  character moment of `f` vanishes.

Conclusion (math): `f` is constant on `G`.
Conclusion (Lean): `f u = f u'`.

Proof body (3 lines): obtains `Fintype (G →* ℂˣ)` from `Fintype.ofFinite`; notes the dual
cardinality `(Fintype.card (G →* ℂˣ) : ℂ) ≠ 0`; then `mul_left_cancel₀` applied to
`(card_mul_eq_sum_of_sum_char_mul_eq_zero f hf u).trans (… u').symm`. I.e. both `f u` and `f u'`
equal the common average `(#(G →* ℂˣ))⁻¹ · ∑ f`, via the file's own engine lemma
`card_mul_eq_sum_of_sum_char_mul_eq_zero`.

Most general standard form: the result is **not specific to ℂ**. The natural statement is over any
commutative ring `R` in which `|G|` (equivalently `Monoid.exponent G`) is invertible and which

closed field of characteristic 0, or more generally `HasEnoughRootsOfUnity R (Monoid.exponent G)`.
This is *exactly the hypothesis cluster mathlib already uses* for finite-abelian duality (see Phase 5).

Generality dimensions where the literature varies:

  - **character idiom**: complex `G →* ℂˣ` / `G → S¹` (classical) vs. additive `AddChar G R`
    (mathlib's harmonic-analysis idiom) vs. `MulChar M R` (mathlib's number-theory idiom).
  - **phrasing**: pointwise "`f` constant" vs. algebraic "an element of `R[G]` with vanishing
    nontrivial isotypic components lies in the line of the trivial idempotent (the all-ones span)".
