target residue `a ∈ ℤ/c`. Then there exist real constants `κ` (a leading density) and `C'` (an error

#### GRS, JNT 243 (2023), Theorem 1 — verbatim (the literature-standard form)

> **Theorem 1.** Let `q` be an integral ideal of `K` and `[b]` be an element of `H_q(K)`. For any real
> number `x ≥ 1`, we have
>
>  `∑_{a ⊂ O_K, [a]=[b], Na ≤ x} 1  =  (α_K φ(q) / (|H_q(K)| Nq)) · x  +  O*( E(K) F(q) n_K log(3F(q)) · (x/Nq)^{1 − 1/n_K} )`
>
> where `F(q) = 2^{r_1} φ(q) h_K / h_{K,q}` and `E(K) = …` [explicit in `n_K, R_K, |μ_K|`]. … When
> `q = O_K`, the group `H_q(K)` is the usual class group in the narrow sense.

(The paper additionally notes Tatuzawa [11] proved the same shape with `K`-dependence not controlled,
and that J. Sunley's earlier Landau-method result has the **sharper** `x`-exponent `1 − 2/(n_K+1)`.)

### Literature summary (Phase 3)

Concept identified as: **the effective ideal-counting theorem in classes of the (narrow) ray class
group** — count `#{N(a) ≤ x, [a]=[b]} = (density)·x + O(x^{1−1/d})`. Classical (Hecke/Weber/Landau),
canonically attributed to **Tatuzawa (1973)** for the ray-class version, with the modern fully-explicit
treatment in **Gun–Ramaré–Sivaraman, JNT 243 (2023)** (the file's cited source).
Sources agree on the standard form: **yes** — main term linear in `x` with a class-number-formula
density coefficient; error a power of `x` with a degree-dependent exponent.
Most general standard form: the count is taken over a class `[b]` of the **narrow ray class group
`H_q(K)` modulo an integral ideal `q`** (the finest natural grouping); `q = O_K` recovers the narrow
class group. The leading constant is `α_K φ(q)/(|H_q(K)| Nq)` (`α_K` = residue of `ζ_K` at 1).
Generality dimensions where the literature varies:
  - **Grouping**: from total count (Landau ideal theorem) → narrow class group → **narrow ray class
    group `H_q(K)`** (the most refined). Our decl groups by **norm-residue `N(I) ≡ a (mod c)`**, which
    is *strictly coarser* than `H_q(K)`: it is a union of ray classes, recoverable by summing the
    finer counts. So our grouping sits **below** the literature standard.
  - **Error exponent**: `1 − 1/d` (Weber / Tatuzawa / GRS) vs. the sharper `1 − 2/(d+1)` (Landau /
    Sunley). Our decl uses **`1 − 1/d`** — the weaker, classical exponent.
  - **Constant**: the literature *names* the leading constant explicitly (class-number-formula shape).
    Our decl leaves it as an **existential `∃ κ`** — it does not identify `κ` (and does not need to,
    since `tendsto_div_atTop_of_sub_mul_rpow_le` later pins `κ` as `lim count/N`).
Disagreement with the literature: none mathematically — our statement is a true, weaker (coarser
grouping, existential constant, classical exponent) specialisation of the standard theorem.
