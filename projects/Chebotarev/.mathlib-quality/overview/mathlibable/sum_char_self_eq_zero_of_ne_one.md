### Statement (Phase 1)

`sum_char_self_eq_zero_of_ne_one` is a **theorem** stating the following:

> Let `G` be a finite commutative group and let `χ : G → ℂˣ` be a complex character of `G` (a
> group homomorphism into the units of `ℂ`). If `χ` is nontrivial (`χ ≠ 1`), then the sum of its
> values over the whole group vanishes: `∑_{g ∈ G} χ(g) = 0` (the values `χ(g) ∈ ℂˣ` cast to `ℂ`).

This is one of the two classical **orthogonality relations** for characters of a finite abelian group —
specifically **"row orthogonality"** (the sum of a fixed nontrivial character over the group is zero),
dual to the "column orthogonality" `sum_char_apply_eq_zero_of_ne_one` proved just above it in the same file.

Variables / typeclasses involved (Lean side):
- `{G : Type*}` — the group.
- `[CommGroup G]` — `G` is a (multiplicative) commutative group.
- `[Fintype G]` — `G` is finite.
- `{χ : G →* ℂˣ}` — a multiplicative character: a monoid/group hom from `G` to `ℂˣ`.

Hypotheses (Lean side):
- `(hχ : χ ≠ 1)` — `χ` is not the trivial (principal) character.

Conclusion (math): `∑_{g ∈ G} χ(g) = 0`.
Conclusion (Lean): `∑ g : G, ((χ g : ℂˣ) : ℂ) = 0`.

**Proof recorded (for the composition analysis).** The source proof is the standard translation trick,
factored through a private helper `sum_eq_zero_of_mulLeft_mul_const_aux`:
pick `g₀` with `χ g₀ ≠ 1` (`DFunLike.ne_iff`), observe `χ(g₀·g) = χ(g₀)·χ(g)`, so left-translation by `g₀`
scales the sum by `χ(g₀) ≠ 1`; a sum fixed under multiplication by a non-`1` cancellable scalar is `0`
(`eq_zero_of_mul_eq_self_left`). Crucially the helper only needs `IsRightCancelMulZero` on the target and a
group structure on the index — **not** commutativity of `G`.

### Literature summary (Phase 3)

Concept identified as: **row orthogonality relation for characters of a finite (abelian) group** — equivalently
the 1-dimensional case of the **Schur orthogonality relations**; in number theory, the orthogonality of a
**Dirichlet character** over `(ℤ/mℤ)ˣ`.

Sources agree on the standard form: **yes** — every source gives
`∑_{g∈G} χ(g) = |G|·[χ = χ₀]`, i.e. `0` for nontrivial `χ`.

Most general standard form (synthesised from sources + the proof): for a **finite group `G`** (abelian *not*
required) and a homomorphism `χ : G → Rˣ` into the units of an integral domain `R` (or more sharply: any `R`
in which `χ(g₀) − 1` is a non-zero-divisor for some `g₀`), if `χ ≠ 1` then `∑_{g∈G} χ(g) = 0`. The complex
case `R = ℂ` is the classical instance; the proof is the translation trick and uses **none** of: commutativity
of `G`, the field structure of `ℂ`, or roots-of-unity — only finiteness, a homomorphism, and cancellability
of `χ(g₀) − 1`.

Generality dimensions where the literature varies:
  - **codomain**: `ℂˣ` (classical) → roots of unity → `Rˣ` for `R` an integral domain. Most general: `R` a domain.
  - **group**: stated for abelian in elementary treatments, but the identity (sum of a *single* character) holds
    for **any** finite group — abelianness is only needed for the *column* relation, not this *row* one.

Disagreement with the literature: none. The user's form is the classical instance of a strictly more general
standard fact.
