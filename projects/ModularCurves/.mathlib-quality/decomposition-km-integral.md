## KM's own proof architecture (this IS the tree — transcribed, not invented)

**Reduction 1 (p. 129, §5.2):** "By 3.7.1, the theorem is true over ℤ[1/N]. In view of the
'factorization into prime powers' (3.5.1) of all the moduli problems involved, it suffices
to treat the case when N is a power of a single prime p." → leaves [KM-W0-3] (3.5.1
factorization) + the landed ℤ[1/N] theory.

**Reduction 2 — Deligne's homogeneity principle (pp. 129–130):** axioms Reg. 1–4 for a fixed
prime p:
> "Reg. 1. 𝒫 is relatively representable and finite over (Ell).
>  Reg. 2. 𝒫⊗ℤ[1/p] is finite etale over (Ell/ℤ[1/p]).
>  Reg. 3. 𝒫 depends only on the underlying p-divisible group […]
>  Reg. 4. Let k be an algebraically closed field of characteristic p, E₀/k a supersingular
>  elliptic curve, and E/W(k)[[T]] its universal formal deformation. Then (4A) 𝒫(E₀/k)
>  consists of a single element, and (4B) the (consequently local) finite W[[T]]-scheme
>  𝒫_{E/W[[T]]} is the spectrum of a regular two-dimensional local ring."

**Axiomatic Regularity Theorem 5.2.1 (p. 130, verbatim):** "Any moduli problem 𝒫 satisfying
the axioms Reg. 1 through Reg. 4 is finite and flat over (Ell) of constant rank ≥ 1, and
regular (necessarily of dimension two)."
Proof structure (pp. 130–134, read at source): auxiliary rigid étale level δ₁ (naive full
level ℓ, ℓ ≠ p odd; 𝔐(δ₁) smooth ℤ-curve by 4.7.1); the open set 𝒰 ⊆ 𝔐(δ₁) of
regular-and-flat points (complement closed by properness of finite π); Reg 2 ⟹ 𝒰 ⊇ [1/p];
strict-henselization + completion bridge (EGA IV 18.8.8, 18.8.13) + the (*) fibre-product
spectrum identity; Serre–Tate + Reg 3 ⟹ 𝒫_{E/W[[T]]} depends only on E₀[p^∞]; the k̄
dichotomy (μ_{p^∞}×ℚ_p/ℤ_p ordinary vs the height-2 formal group supersingular) ⟹ up to
W[[T]]-iso only TWO such schemes ⟹ homogeneity (H1)/(H2); Reg 4 seeds the supersingular
class; 4.12.1 gives rank constancy.

**Prop 5.2.2 (Reg 4B bis; p. 135, verbatim):** "𝒫_{E/W[[T]]} is the spectrum of a local ring
whose maximal ideal is generated by two elements" suffices — dimension ≥ 2 from finiteness +
dominance ("its image is closed (the map being finite, hence proper) and its image contains
the open dense set Spec((W[[T]])⊗ℤ[1/p])"), then "It is then automatically flat over
W[[T]], because any finite morphism between regular schemes of the same dimension is
automatically flat [A-K 1, V, 3.6]." ← miracle flatness for regular schemes: **mathlib GAP**.

> "(5.3.2.2) (Rigid II). Let k be an algebraically closed field of characteristic p. If R is
> an artin local W(k)-algebra, and if E/R admits 0 as a point of 'exact order pⁿ', then R is

**Lemma 5.3.1, Γ₁-case (p. 136, verbatim):** "Consider next 𝒫 = [Γ₁(N)]. Then 𝒫(E₀/k) is
