## The missing Cartier representability block

Your Definition 4.3 takes a relative effective Cartier divisor in a smooth curve as,
operationally, a finite locally free closed subscheme, with the official definition
deferred behind a line-bundle API. That is a good working shortcut, but it should be
split into an explicit mini-project, because Katz–Mazur use more than finite local
freeness: they use ideal sheaves, sums, pullbacks, section divisors, degree, and zero
loci of restricted sections.

Katz–Mazur's official definition is: a relative effective Cartier divisor D ⊂ X is a
closed subscheme flat over S whose ideal sheaf is invertible; affine-locally it is cut
out by a non-zero-divisor f with A/fA flat over the base. They then identify such
divisors with pairs (L, s) consisting of an invertible sheaf and a section, and
addition of divisors corresponds to tensor product of these pairs.

For smooth curves, Katz–Mazur prove that sections give effective Cartier divisors, and
that finite flat closed subschemes of finite presentation in a smooth curve are
effective Cartier divisors; conversely, proper effective Cartier divisors in smooth
curves are finite flat. Degree is then the finite locally free rank, is additive under
sums, and is preserved by base change.

The key lemma is the incidence lemma: for a smooth curve C/S and effective Cartier
divisors D, D', with D' proper over S, there is a closed subscheme Z ⊂ S universal for
D' ≤ D; it is locally cut out by deg(D') equations and commutes with arbitrary base
change. The proof is exactly "restrict the section defining D to D' and set its
coordinates equal to zero" in the finite locally free module H⁰(D', L|_{D'}).

So I would add these tickets before exact-order and Γ(N):

    ecd-pair-section-interface
    ecd-base-change-and-flat-pullback
    smooth-curve-section-divisors
    finite-flat-subscheme-is-ecd
    ecd-degree
    zero-locus-section-flf-module
    incidenceLE

    subgroup-divisor-locus

The "subgroup divisor" ticket is especially important. Katz–Mazur prove that the
condition "D is a subgroup" is a closed condition by reducing it to three divisor
conditions: [e] ≤ D, D = inv*(D), and [m(P₁,P₂)] ≤ D over D ×_S D. The locus is locally
cut out by 1 + deg(D) + deg(D)² equations and commutes with base change.

Once this exists, exact order and Drinfeld level are formal. Katz–Mazur define exact
order by saying that [P] + [2P] + ⋯ + [NP] is a subgroup divisor; cyclic subgroups are
fppf-locally generated by such a point. And the general A-structure functor is
represented by the closed subscheme of Hom(A,C) on which the universal divisor
Σ[φ_univ(a)] is a subgroup.

## Answers to the planner's questions

### Q1. Definition of record

I would **not** make Definition 4.1 the permanent definition of record. … I would
instead use two records: EllipticCurveGeom (E, π, zero, isSection, smooth, proper,
relDimOne, geomConnectedGenusOneFibres — opaque/black-box at first) and, for immediate
modular-curve work, EllipticCurve extends EllipticCurveGeom with (group :
CommGroupSchemeOver S E, zero_is_identity). Mathematically the group law is redundant,
but it is the object Katz–Mazur Chapter 1 actually needs: a smooth commutative group
scheme of relative dimension one. Later, prove that EllipticCurveGeom canonically gives
EllipticCurve via Picard/Abel. This avoids making the whole level-structure theory wait
for Pic⁰.

### Q2. Residue-field versus geometric-fibre condition

Prefer the standard geometric-fibre formulation in the eventual theorem statement. If
you keep the residue-field Weierstrass condition as a bridge, phrase it as: for each s,
there exists a pointed isomorphism of κ(s)-schemes E_s ≅ Proj(Weierstrass cubic over
κ(s)). I would avoid "points over every field extension are naturally identified,"
because that is a functor-of-points formulation and is more awkward in Lean than an
isomorphism of schemes. I do not see a fundamental imperfect-field pathology if the
Weierstrass model is genuinely smooth over κ(s). The real issue is API stability, not
correctness.

### Q3. Group-law route

I would **not** endorse the Abel/Pic⁰ route as the first route. Use group law as data
now; prove canonicity later. If you do take the Abel/Pic⁰ route, the black boxes should
be very explicit and should not grow: coherent-base-change; relative-duality-genus-one;
relative-Picard; Poincare; Abel-isomorphism; group-law-from-Abel; torsion. But I would
keep all of that as a later "purity/comparison" project.

### Q4. Registered-data discipline

The discipline is fine if the pinning specifications include **base-change/naturality**,
not only fibrewise comparison. Over nonreduced bases, equality of morphisms is not
generally detected on geometric fibres. For registered data, require: compatible with
arbitrary base change; functorial under isomorphism of elliptic curves; satisfies the
universal property defining it; agrees with the classical construction over fields. For
V_ρ̄, the pinning spec should be the finite-étale/Galois equivalence, not merely a
description of geometric points.

### Q5. Weil pairing construction

For the final API, I would prefer **Cartier duality/autoduality**. … with bilinearity,
alternatingness, perfectness when N is invertible, base-change compatibility, and
compatibility with N ∣ M. For composite N, duality is also cleaner. A norm/divisor
construction can be retained as the field-comparison theorem or as the
Katz–Mazur-compatible proof backend. For the immediate Y(ρ̄) target over characteristic
zero, a finite-étale/Galois-descent version of the classical Weil pairing is an
acceptable first milestone, provided the eventual scheme-level construction has the
same API.

### Q6. Normalisation

Adopt the Silverman convention: with σ(ζ) = ζ^{χ(σ)} the determinant of the mod-N
Galois representation on E[N] is χ. Pin it by e_N(aP + bQ, cP + dQ) = e_N(P,Q)^{ad−bc}
and by Galois equivariance e_N(σP, σQ) = σ(e_N(P,Q)). This convention matters because
Katz's notes decompose full-level moduli over Z[1/n, ζ_n] into components indexed by
primitive nth roots occurring as values of the pairing on the chosen basis.

### Q7. Moduli formalism versus stacks

Your chosen packaging is honest for the first target, but I would slightly strengthen
it. … However, do not make everything set-valued too early. Internally, keep the
groupoid-valued moduli problem S ↦ groupoid of elliptic curves over S with level
structure. Then specialize to a set-valued functor only after rigidification, e.g. full
level N ≥ 3, or after proving automorphisms are killed. This will save you pain when
you later touch X₀(N), coarse moduli, or quotients.

### Q8. Staging over Z[1/N]

For Y(ρ̄) over Q, doing the first phase over Z[1/N] or characteristic zero is fine. For
Mazur-style and semistable-reduction consumers, the earliest integral statements are
likely: E[N] finite locally free of rank N² over arbitrary base; E[N] finite étale when
N is invertible; exact-order and cyclic-subgroup definitions by Cartier divisors; Γ₀(N)
as finite locally free cyclic subgroup schemes; N-isog moduli problem; finite flatness
of degeneracy maps; regularity/semistable structure for Γ₀(p) with auxiliary prime-to-p
level; cusps and compactification only after the open problem is stable. So I would
change the ticket board to make N-Isog, cyclicity as a closed/fppf-local condition, and
finite-group quotients explicit intermediate phases, not just later KM-gated leaves.

### Q9. The twisted curve Y(ρ̄)

The twist idea is right, but I would be careful about the pairing component. …
GL₂(Z/N) acts on the pairing by determinant; fixing p : Λ²ρ ≃ μ_N makes the target a
symplectic finite étale group scheme. The slicker and safer route is to define the
twisted problem directly: for T/Q, classify elliptic curves E/T plus α : E[N] ≅ V_{ρ̄,T}
such that α carries the Weil pairing on E[N] to p_T. Then prove this functor is
represented by the corresponding symplectic Isom scheme Isom^symp(E[N], V_ρ̄) over the
full-level moduli stack or over a rigidified level base. This gives the moduli
interpretation by construction. Afterward prove it is the same as the Galois twist of
the appropriate full-level curve/component.
