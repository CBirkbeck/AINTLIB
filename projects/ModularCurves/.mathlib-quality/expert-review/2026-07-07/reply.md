## Highest-priority correction: the `Γ₁(N)` definition

The brief currently says a Drinfeld `Γ₁(N)` structure is a section `P` of `E[N]` such that `(aP)_{a∈ℤ/N}` is a full set of sections of `E[N]`, with a caveat that the code uses `Σ_a [aP] = E[N]`. That cannot be right as stated: `Σ_a [aP]` has degree `N`, whereas `E[N]` has rank/degree `N²`.

The correct Katz–Mazur/Drinfeld distinction is:

```
Γ₁(N): P has exact order N iff the degree-N divisor Σ_{a mod N} [aP] is a subgroup divisor of E.
Γ(N):  (P,Q) is full level iff the degree-N² divisor Σ_{a,b mod N} [aP+bQ] equals E[N] as a Cartier divisor.
```

Katz–Mazur's introduction states full Drinfeld level as the equality `E[N] = Σ[aP+bQ]` as a Cartier divisor, but Deligne's `Γ₁(N)` exact-order condition is that `Σ[aP]` is a subgroup scheme, not that it equals all of `E[N]`. Their cyclic `Γ₀(N)` condition is similarly that a rank-`N` subgroup is fppf-locally generated by `P` with `G = Σ[aP]`.

Proposed inventory statements (IsExactOrder via IsSubgroupDivisor; Gamma1Level = {P // N•P=0 ∧ IsExactOrder P N}; FullLevel = {P Q // divisorSum over (ZMod N)² = torsionDivisor E N}). If the current code already has the correct divisor degree internally, the prose needs correction. If the code really equates the `Γ₁` divisor with `E[N]`, fix the code before more downstream statements depend on it.

## Quotient stack: correct direction, but finite quotients are not enough

`M_ell^W := [U/G]` with `U` = affine scheme of nonsingular Weierstrass equations, `G` = coordinate-change group, is right (KM work with stacks "without ever saying so", via relatively representable moduli problems).

Structural gap: the Weierstrass coordinate-change group is NOT finite constant. It is an affine group scheme, morally `G = G_m ⋉ A³` with coordinates `(u,r,s,t)`, `u` invertible. The `Q` stream's finite invariant quotient `Spec(A^G)` is useful for finite group quotients later (`GL₂(ℤ/N)` quotients, KM Ch. 7) but is NOT the quotient-stack machinery for `[U/G]`. KM's TOC also separates finite-group quotients as a later major topic, not the Weierstrass atlas construction itself.

Split the workstream:
- W-stack: affine group scheme actions, G-torsors, quotient groupoid `[U/G]`.
- Q-finite: finite constant group actions, affine invariant quotients, finite quotients of level schemes.

Do not wait for coarse affine invariants to finish `[U/G]`.

## T-A4 re-freeze: replace by coordinate-change uniqueness, but be careful about the formula

The old T-A4 was correctly found false; the counterexample and proposed re-freeze are the right kind of adversarial catch. I endorse replacing T-A4 by a coordinate-change statement, but formulate in terms of the **Weierstrass coordinate-change group**, not by hard-coding `x ↦ x+a, y ↦ y+ax+b` until KM is checked very literally.

Safe replacement:
```
For a fixed locally-Weierstrass elliptic curve E/S, the sheaf of Weierstrass presentations of E
is a torsor under the Weierstrass coordinate-change group G. After choosing a trivialisation of
the Hodge/conormal line, the remaining coordinate changes are exactly the subgroup with u = 1.
```
In coordinates the general change is `x = u²x' + r`, `y = u³y' + u²s x' + t`. Choosing a differential trivialisation fixes the `u`-factor. Whether KM 2.2.5 further reduces to two parameters depends on exactly what "adapted" includes. Make the Lean theorem consume the group-action statement rather than a fragile coordinate formula. For `ω`, use a chosen trivialisation of the Hodge/conormal sheaf at the identity (compatible with the modular-forms side where Katz defines `ω_{E/S}` via differentials and Serre duality).

## Weil pairing: your proposed route is right, with a caveat

Agree: first characteristic-zero finite-étale descent pairing; later KM 2.8 Cartier-divisor construction over arbitrary bases. The F-stream Galois correspondence gives the descent heart (Galois-equivariant map on geometric fibres descends to a finite étale morphism); `exists_pairingAlgebraHom_of_galoisEquivariant` is the right abstraction.

Caveat: a pairing over **fields** is not enough for the moduli functor on arbitrary `ℚ`-schemes `T`. `Y(ρ̄)` classifies elliptic curves over `T`, not just over fields. The char-0 route should target a `T`-relative morphism `weilPairingCharZero : E[N] ×_T E[N] ⟶ μ_{N,T}` over `Spec ℚ`-schemes, constructed étale-locally on `T` (where finite étale group schemes trivialize) then descended. If the first milestone only proves the field case, label it a field-valued-points theorem, not the pairing needed for `RepresentsYRho`. Keep the Silverman/determinant normalization; the symplectic pin `e_N(aP+bQ,cP+dQ) = e_N(P,Q)^{ad-bc}` is the correct kind of project-wide spec.

## Galois correspondence: upstreaming advice

For mathlib-facing generality: yes, parameterize the fibre functor by an arbitrary separably closed geometric point `Ω/k` with an `IsSepClosure k Ω` (or equivalent) hypothesis, rather than hard-coding the separable closure — composes better with existing algebraic-closure APIs and user-chosen universes. For the decomposition result, upstream through the standard theorem `finite étale k-algebra ≃ finite product of finite separable field extensions`, then derive `connected finite étale k-algebra ↔ field`. The idempotent-splitting proof is fine, but mathlib benefits more from the reusable primitive-idempotent/product decomposition.
