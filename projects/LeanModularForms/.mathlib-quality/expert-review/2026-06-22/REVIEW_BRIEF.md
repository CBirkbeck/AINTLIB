# Review brief — the integral structure of the Hecke algebra on $S_k(\Gamma_1(N))$

*Prepared 2026-06-22 for a mathematician expert in modular forms / arithmetic geometry.
Self-contained: no formalization-system knowledge required. We are formalizing the arithmetic of
Hecke eigenvalues in a proof assistant and have reduced the entire development to a **single
classical input**; we want guidance on the **most economical route to prove that input**, because
the route choice dominates the remaining effort.*

## 1. Goal and the one remaining input

We work with holomorphic cusp forms $S_k(\Gamma_1(N))$, integer weight $k \ge 2$, level $N \ge 1$,
over $\mathbb C$. Let $\mathbb T_{\mathbb Z} \subseteq \operatorname{End}_{\mathbb C}(S_k(\Gamma_1(N)))$
be the $\mathbb Z$-subalgebra generated by all Hecke operators $T_n$ ($n \ge 1$) and all diamond
operators $\langle d\rangle$ ($d \in (\mathbb Z/N)^\times$). (At primes $p \mid N$, $T_p = U_p$.)

**Everything in our development now rests on one statement**, which we have isolated and which we
would like the cleanest possible proof of:

> **(FIH)** $\mathbb T_{\mathbb Z}$ is a finitely generated $\mathbb Z$-module.

We have proved (formally, sorry-free) that **(FIH) follows from** the existence of a lattice:

> **(L)** There is a $\mathbb Z$-submodule $\Lambda \subseteq S_k(\Gamma_1(N))$ that is
> (i) free of finite rank, (ii) **of full rank** ($\Lambda \otimes_{\mathbb Z}\mathbb C = S_k(\Gamma_1(N))$),
> and (iii) **stable** under every $T_n$ and $\langle d\rangle$.

Indeed, given (L): each $T \in \mathbb T_{\mathbb Z}$ restricts to a $\mathbb Z$-endomorphism of
$\Lambda$; the restriction map $\mathbb T_{\mathbb Z}\to \operatorname{End}_{\mathbb Z}(\Lambda)\cong
M_r(\mathbb Z)$ is injective (full rank $\Rightarrow$ an operator vanishing on $\Lambda$ vanishes on
$\Lambda\otimes\mathbb C = S_k$); a sub-$\mathbb Z$-module of the finite free $M_r(\mathbb Z)$ is
finite. **This endgame is done.** So the entire question is: **prove (L)** (or prove (FIH) by any
route).

**Downstream of (FIH), also already proved formally:** the Fourier coefficients $a_n(f)$ of a
normalized newform are algebraic integers; the coefficient field $K_f=\mathbb Q(a_n(f):n)$ is a
number field. **Independent of (FIH) and also already proved:** a newform is a simultaneous
eigenform for *all* $T_n$ (Atkin–Lehner / multiplicity one), and the eigensystem
$\mathbb T_{\mathbb Z}\to\mathbb C$ is a ring homomorphism.

## 2. What infrastructure is and is not available

This is the crux for judging "economy of route." The proof assistant's library currently has:

* $\mathrm{SL}_2(\mathbb Z)$, $\Gamma_1(N)$, the upper half-plane $\mathbb H$ and the weight-$k$ slash action;
* the space $S_k(\Gamma_1(N))$ and the fact $\dim_{\mathbb C} S_k(\Gamma_1(N)) < \infty$;
* $q$-expansions, and the **explicit Hecke action on Fourier coefficients with integer coefficients**:
  $a_m(T_n f)=\sum_{d\mid(m,n)} d^{k-1}\chi(d)\,a_{mn/d^2}(f)$ (and the $U_p$ formula at $p\mid N$);
* the Petersson inner product and basic operations;
* basic group (co)homology (`H^*` of a group with coefficients in a module) at a foundational level;
* Eisenstein series $E_4,E_6$ and (level 1) the structure of the graded ring of modular forms.

It currently has **none** of: modular curves $X_1(N)$ as Riemann surfaces or schemes; their (integral)
homology/cohomology; modular symbols / Manin symbols; the Eichler–Shimura isomorphism or period map;
the algebraic theory of modular forms over $\mathbb Z$ / the $q$-expansion principle; Riemann–Roch.

## 3. The routes we know, and their apparent cost

* **(A) Eichler–Shimura / modular symbols.** Build the integral modular-symbol module
  $\mathbb M_k(\Gamma_1(N);\mathbb Z)$ (Manin presentation: generators indexed by
  $\Gamma_1(N)\backslash\mathrm{SL}_2(\mathbb Z)\times \operatorname{Sym}^{k-2}\mathbb Z^2$, with the
  2-term, 3-term and parabolic relations) — finite free and Hecke-stable (Heilbronn matrices). Then
  the period pairing $\langle f,\{\alpha,\beta\}\otimes P\rangle=\int_\alpha^\beta f(z)P(z,1)\,dz$
  gives a Hecke-equivariant map $S_k \to \mathbb M_k^\vee\otimes\mathbb C$, **injective** (a cusp form
  with all periods zero is zero). Pulling back the lattice $\mathbb M_k(\mathbb Z)^\vee$ gives (L).
  *Apparent cost:* build modular symbols + the analytic period integral + injectivity — three
  substantial pieces.
* **(B) $q$-expansion principle / rationality.** Take $\Lambda=\{f\in S_k:\,a_n(f)\in\mathbb Z\ \forall n\}$
  (or over $\mathbb Z[\zeta_N]$ to absorb $\chi$). Hecke-stability (iii) is immediate from the integer
  Fourier formula above (we have it). The hard part is full rank (ii): *$S_k(\Gamma_1(N))$ has a
  $\mathbb C$-basis of forms with integer $q$-expansions.* Standard proofs go through Katz's algebraic
  modular forms over $\mathbb Z$, or through (A).
* **(C) Riemann-surface homology (Diamond–Shurman, weight 2 only).** $H_1(X_1(N),\mathbb Z)\hookrightarrow
  S_2^\vee$; the lattice is the integral homology of the modular curve. Restricted to $k=2$, and DS
  cites the existence/rank/full-rankness from Riemann-surface theory without proof.

Diamond–Shurman, Shimura (Thm 3.48, 3.51, 3.52, and §8.4), and Miyake (Thm 4.5.9, 4.5.19) all prove
the *endgame* we already have, but each *imports* the lattice (DS from Riemann-surface homology;
Shimura defers (3.5.20) to the §8.4 cohomology of cusp forms). None gives an elementary, substrate-free
construction of (L) at general weight $k$.

## 4. Questions for the reviewer

1. **Cheapest route given §2.** With exactly the infrastructure listed in §2 available and the
   substrate of §2's second paragraph *absent*, which route to (FIH)/(L) minimizes the new
   foundational mathematics that must be built? Concretely: is (A) modular symbols, (B) the
   $q$-expansion principle, or something else, the least-prerequisite path?

2. **Exploiting the weak target.** We need only (FIH) — equivalently a *faithful integral
   representation* of $\mathbb T_{\mathbb Z}$, equivalently the full-rank stable lattice (L). We do
   **not** need the full Eichler–Shimura isomorphism $\mathbb M_k^\vee\otimes\mathbb C\cong S_k\oplus
   \overline{S_k}\oplus\mathrm{Eis}$. In route (A), can one avoid the analytic period integral and
   the surjectivity/Hodge-decomposition half, getting away with only: finiteness + Hecke-stability of
   $\mathbb M_k(\mathbb Z)$, plus *injectivity* of $S_k\to\mathbb M_k^\vee\otimes\mathbb C$? Is that
   injectivity ("non-zero cusp form has a non-zero period") substantially cheaper than the full
   comparison, and what is its most elementary proof?

3. **Is rationality avoidable-by-cohomology-only, or genuinely independent?** Does every proof of
   "$S_k(\Gamma_1(N))$ has an integer-$q$-expansion basis" (route B's full-rank step) ultimately route
   through either Eichler–Shimura cohomology or algebraic geometry over $\mathbb Z$? Or is there a
   self-contained analytic/combinatorial proof (e.g., via Hecke-stability + a dimension/Sturm
   argument) that a proof assistant could reach without modular curves?

4. **Sturm bound as a shortcut to (FIH).** A Sturm bound says a form in $S_k(\Gamma_1(N))$ is
   determined by its first $B \sim \tfrac{k}{12}[\mathrm{SL}_2(\mathbb Z):\Gamma_1(N)]$ Fourier
   coefficients, so $S_k \hookrightarrow \mathbb C^{B}$ via $f\mapsto(a_1,\dots,a_B)$. Combined with the
   *integer* Fourier–Hecke recursion, does this give a direct proof that $\mathbb T_{\mathbb Z}$ is
   module-finite — e.g. that $\mathbb T_{\mathbb Z}$ embeds into the integer matrices acting on the
   integral span of the coefficient vectors — **without** first proving a full-rank integral basis
   exists? If not, precisely where does the argument break (the image $\{(a_n(f))\}$ need not be a
   *full-rank* integral lattice without rationality)?

5. **Bypassing the lattice for the actual conclusions.** Our ultimate targets are only: (a) eigenvalues
   are algebraic integers, (b) $[K_f:\mathbb Q]<\infty$. Is there a route to (a)+(b) that avoids the
   integral lattice entirely — e.g. bounding $[\,\mathbb Q(a_n:n):\mathbb Q]$ directly by
   $r=\dim_{\mathbb C}S_k$ using only the integer Hecke recursion and the commuting/diagonalizability
   of $\{T_n\}_{(n,N)=1}$? (Algebraicity over $\mathbb Q$ of eigenvalues is easy from finite dimension;
   the integrality over $\mathbb Z$ is the part that seems to need the integral structure — is that
   right?)

6. **Existing formalizations.** Are you aware of any formalization (Lean/mathlib, Coq, Isabelle, or
   elsewhere) of modular symbols, the integral Hecke algebra, the Eichler–Shimura map, or the
   $q$-expansion principle, that could be reused or imitated?

## 5. What a useful answer looks like

A ranking of the routes by formalization prerequisite, a pointer to the most elementary proof of
whichever sub-step is the true bottleneck (we suspect: *injectivity of the period map* in (A), or
*rationality* in (B)), and a verdict on whether the Sturm-bound shortcut (Q4) or the
bypass-the-lattice idea (Q5) is viable or illusory. Even "all routes genuinely require building
$X$" is valuable — it tells us the minimal foundational target to commit to.
