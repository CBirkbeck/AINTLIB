# Review brief — Hecke eigenvalue arithmetic for a formalised newform-label project

*Prepared 2026-06-19 for a senior modular-forms / arithmetic-geometry expert. Self-contained: no
code or repository access required. The setting is a computer formalisation (in the Lean theorem
prover) of the theory of modular forms; the questions are pure mathematics, but the constraint
"what can actually be built from current foundations" shapes which proof we want.*

## 1. Goal

We have formalised the construction of canonical LMFDB-style labels for newforms of level `N` and
weight `k`, together with the proof that the labelling is well-defined and injective on Galois
orbits. That development is complete and rigorous **except** that it rests on two classical inputs
about the arithmetic of Hecke eigenvalues which we have so far left unproven:

- **(A) Integrality.** For a normalised newform `f ∈ S_k(Γ₁(N))` with `q`-expansion
  `f = ∑_{n≥1} a_n(f) q^n`, `a_1(f)=1`, each coefficient `a_n(f)` is an **algebraic integer**.
- **(B) Number field.** The **coefficient field** `K_f := ℚ(a_n(f) : n ≥ 1) ⊆ ℂ` is a **number
  field**, i.e. `[K_f : ℚ] < ∞`.

Both are textbook facts (Shimura, *Introduction to the Arithmetic Theory of Automorphic Functions*,
Thm 3.52; Deligne). Our question is **not** whether they are true, but **what is the lightest
mathematical input from which they can be derived**, given what a formalisation can currently lean
on. Concretely: **must we build (a form of) the `q`-expansion principle / an integral model of the
modular curve `X₁(N)`, or is there an elementary / lighter-weight route?**

## 2. Background and references

### 2.1 Setting and conventions

- `Γ₁(N) ⊆ SL₂(ℤ)` is the usual congruence subgroup; `S_k(Γ₁(N))` is the space of holomorphic
  weight-`k` cusp forms, viewed inside the holomorphic functions on the upper half-plane, with
  `q`-expansion `f = ∑_{n≥1} a_n(f) q^n`, `q = e^{2πi z}`, at the cusp `∞`.
- `S_k(Γ₁(N)) = ⊕_{χ} S_k(N,χ)` decomposes under the diamond operators `⟨d⟩` (`d ∈ (ℤ/N)^×`) into
  nebentypus eigenspaces `S_k(N,χ)`, `χ : (ℤ/N)^× → ℂ^×`.
- **Hecke operators.** For `n` coprime to `N`, `T_n` acts on `S_k(N,χ)`; on `q`-expansions, for
  `f ∈ S_k(N,χ)`,
  ```
  a_m(T_n f) = ∑_{ d | gcd(m,n),  gcd(d,N)=1 }  d^{k-1} · χ(d) · a_{mn/d²}(f).
  ```
  We have this coefficient formula formalised and available. A **newform** is a normalised
  (`a_1=1`) new eigenform; for it `a_n(f)` equals the `T_n`-eigenvalue (for `n` coprime to `N`), and
  the eigenvalues at `n` not coprime to `N` are determined by the standard recursions.
- `K_f = ℚ(a_n : n)`. Equivalently `K_f` is generated by `{a_p : p` prime`}`, and (via the Hecke
  recursions, in which the only "new" quantities are `χ(p)` — a root of unity — and `p^{k-1}`) by
  `{a_p}` together with the `N`-th roots of unity.

### 2.2 References

- [Shimura 1971] G. Shimura, *Introduction to the Arithmetic Theory of Automorphic Functions*,
  Princeton, 1971 — Thm 3.48–3.52 (integrality of eigenvalues; the Hecke ring acts on an integral
  structure coming from the homology of the modular curve).
- [DS 2005] F. Diamond, J. Shurman, *A First Course in Modular Forms*, GTM 228, Springer — §5.x
  (Hecke operators, newforms), §6.x (the integral / rational structure, `q`-expansion principle).
- [Miyake 2006] T. Miyake, *Modular Forms*, Springer Monographs — §4.5–4.6.
- [Katz 1973] N. Katz, "p-adic properties of modular schemes and modular forms", in *Modular
  Functions of One Variable III*, LNM 350 — geometric integral models / `q`-expansion principle.
- [Deligne–Rapoport 1973] P. Deligne, M. Rapoport, "Les schémas de modules de courbes elliptiques",
  same volume — integral models of `X₁(N)`.
- [Eichler–Shimura] the isomorphism relating `S_k` to a piece of `H^1(Γ₁(N), Sym^{k-2}(ℂ²))`, and
  its integral structure via `Sym^{k-2}(ℤ²)`.
- [Manin 1972] Yu. Manin, "Parabolic points and zeta functions of modular curves" — modular symbols,
  an explicit integral structure on which the Hecke operators act.

### 2.3 State of the art

The integrality (A) and number-field (B) statements are completely standard and old. What we are
unsure about is the **minimal foundational footprint** of a *formal* proof. The two textbook routes
we know — (i) the geometric integral model of `X₁(N)` [Katz, Deligne–Rapoport], and (ii) the
homological/cohomological integral structure [Eichler–Shimura, Manin modular symbols] — are both
substantial. We want to know whether one of these is genuinely necessary, which is lighter, or
whether something more elementary suffices for (A)/(B) specifically.

## 3. Strategy (what we have reduced the problem to)

We have reduced both (A) and (B) to a **single** input:

> **(QEP) Hecke-stable full-rank integral structure.** There is a `ℤ`-submodule `L ⊆ S_k(Γ₁(N))`
> (over `ℂ`) that is (i) finitely generated, (ii) of **full rank**, i.e. `L ⊗_ℤ ℂ = S_k(Γ₁(N))`,
> and (iii) stable under all Hecke operators `T_n` (and the diamonds). The natural candidate is
> `L = { f ∈ S_k(Γ₁(N)) : a_n(f) ∈ ℤ for all n }`, the module of integral `q`-expansions.

Given (QEP), the derivations are routine and we are confident we can formalise them:

- **(A) from (QEP).** In a `ℤ`-basis of `L`, each `T_n` is an integer matrix; its characteristic
  polynomial is monic with integer coefficients. Since `L ⊗ ℂ = S_k` (full rank), the newform `f`
  is a `T_n`-eigenvector inside `L ⊗ ℂ` with eigenvalue `a_n(f)`, so `a_n(f)` is a root of that
  monic integer polynomial — an algebraic integer.
- **(B) from (QEP).** The `ℚ`-algebra `𝕋_ℚ` generated by `{T_n}` inside `End_ℚ(L ⊗ ℚ)` is
  finite-dimensional (it is a subalgebra of the endomorphism algebra of a finite-dimensional space).
  The eigensystem `ev_f : 𝕋_ℚ → ℂ`, `T ↦ (T`-eigenvalue on `f)`, is a `ℚ`-algebra homomorphism;
  its image is `ℚ(a_n : n) = K_f`, a finite-dimensional `ℚ`-subalgebra of `ℂ` and hence (being a
  domain) a number field, with `[K_f:ℚ] ≤ dim_ℚ 𝕋_ℚ < ∞`.

We emphasise the asymmetry we noticed: **integrality (A) of the individual `a_n` does *not* by
itself give (B)** — infinitely many algebraic integers can generate an infinite extension. (B)
needs the finite-dimensionality of the Hecke algebra, i.e. a **rational** (not merely "each `a_n`
algebraic") structure. So both (A) and (B) seem to require some form of (QEP); (A) the integral
version, (B) at least the rational version `L ⊗ ℚ` of full rank.

## 4. What is already formalised (so the reviewer knows the foundation)

- `S_k(Γ₁(N))` is **finite-dimensional over `ℂ`** (proved via the dimension/genus computation).
- The explicit `T_n` `q`-expansion coefficient formula of §2.1 is available, as is the diamond
  action; the abstract double-coset Hecke ring and its action on nebentypus eigenspaces are
  formalised.
- Strong multiplicity one and the Atkin–Lehner "main lemma" (a cusp form with vanishing
  prime-to-`N` coefficients is old) are formalised and axiom-clean; in particular the newform
  theory (old/new decomposition, the finite set of newforms) is in place.
- We do **not** have any integral or rational structure on `S_k(Γ₁(N))` — no `ℤ`-lattice, no
  `ℚ`-form, no statement that `T_n` is "defined over `ℚ`". This is precisely the gap.

## 5. Where we are stuck

> **Stuck point.** The candidate `L = {f : a_n(f) ∈ ℤ ∀ n}` is visibly `T_n`-stable (the
> coefficient formula of §2.1 has integer-times-(root-of-unity) coefficients, and the diamonds are
> integral), so (QEP)(iii) is provable from what we have. The obstruction is (QEP)(ii), **full
> rank**: that `L` (or its `ℚ`-analogue) actually *spans* `S_k(Γ₁(N))` — equivalently, that
> `S_k(Γ₁(N))` has a `ℂ`-basis of forms with integer (or rational) `q`-expansions. A priori `L`
> could be much smaller than a full lattice.

The two routes we can see to full rank are both heavy:
- **Geometric.** The integral model of `X₁(N)` and forms as global sections of a line bundle over
  `ℤ[1/N]` (Katz, Deligne–Rapoport). This is a very large prerequisite to formalise.
- **Cohomological.** Eichler–Shimura identifies (a sum involving) `S_k` with `H^1` of `Γ₁(N)` with
  `Sym^{k-2}` coefficients, which carries an evident `ℤ`-structure from `Sym^{k-2}(ℤ²)` and the
  integral group homology; Manin's modular symbols make this explicit, and the Hecke action is
  integral there. This still requires formalising Eichler–Shimura (or at least the modular-symbol
  integral structure plus its comparison with `S_k`).

The "Galois descent" shortcut — define a `ℚ`-structure as the `Gal(ℂ/ℚ)`-fixed forms under
`σ · ∑ a_n q^n = ∑ σ(a_n) q^n` — appears **circular** to us: to know that `σ · f` is again a cusp
form one already needs the rational structure / `q`-expansion principle.

## 6. Open mathematical questions for the reviewer

**Q1 (the central one).** For (A) and (B) as stated, is **some** form of the `q`-expansion principle
/ full-rank integral (or rational) structure on `S_k(Γ₁(N))` genuinely *necessary*, or is there an
elementary route we are missing? In particular, is there a proof of (A) — "each `a_n(f)` is an
algebraic integer" — that does **not** pass through exhibiting an integral structure on the whole
space (e.g. directly from integral relations satisfied by the Hecke operators, from a bound, or from
the abstract double-coset Hecke ring)?

**Q2 (lightest route to full rank).** If full rank is unavoidable, which of the two routes
(geometric integral model vs. cohomological / Eichler–Shimura–Manin modular-symbol integral
structure) has the **lightest prerequisites for a formal development**, and is there a third, more
elementary route to "`S_k(Γ₁(N))` has a basis of integer-`q`-expansion forms" — e.g. an explicit
spanning set with rational `q`-expansions (Eisenstein-series products, theta series,
`η`-quotients), valid for general `N`?

**Q3 (decoupling A and B).** Is the number-field statement (B) obtainable **more cheaply** than the
integrality (A)? For (B) we only need a finite-dimensional **rational** Hecke algebra acting on a
**rational** form of `S_k`. Is there a way to get "`T_n` is defined over `ℚ`" (a `ℚ`-structure
`S_k(ℚ)` with `S_k(ℚ) ⊗ ℂ = S_k`) that is strictly easier than the integral statement — and does
that route then *also* yield (A), or do they genuinely require separate inputs?

**Q4 (restricting the space).** We ultimately care only about the eigenvalues of a single newform
`f` (and its Galois conjugates). Is there a route that builds the integral/rational structure only
on a **Hecke-stable subspace** containing `f` (e.g. the new subspace, or the `𝕋`-span of `f`),
rather than on all of `S_k(Γ₁(N))` — and would that be meaningfully easier?

**Q5 (the cleanest honest formalisation).** Given a foundation that has: finite-dimensionality of
`S_k(Γ₁(N))` over `ℂ`, the explicit `T_n` coefficient formula, the diamond action, newform theory,
and strong multiplicity one — but **no** geometric or cohomological integral model — what would you
regard as the *cleanest single classical input* to isolate (state once, cite, and build (A)+(B)
on)? Is "the integral structure `S_k(Γ₁(N), ℤ)` is a full-rank Hecke-stable lattice" the right
atomic statement, or is there a more fundamental / more easily justified one?

**Q6 (weight/level caveats).** Do (A)/(B) — or the cleanest route to them — require any hypotheses
we should be careful about (`k ≥ 2`; behaviour at `k = 1`; `N` small; trivial vs. non-trivial
nebentypus)? We currently aim for all `k ≥ 2` and all `N ≥ 1`.
