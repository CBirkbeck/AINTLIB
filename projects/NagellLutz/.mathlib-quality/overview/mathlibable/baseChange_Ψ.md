### Statement (Phase 1)

`WeierstrassCurve.baseChange_ψ` states a **naturality / base-change
compatibility** fact for the bivariate `n`-division polynomials `ψₙ` of a
Weierstrass curve, for all `n : ℤ`:

Let `R` be a commutative ring, `W` a Weierstrass curve over `R`, `S` an
`R`-algebra, and let `A`, `B` be `S`-algebras with scalar towers `R → S → A`
and `R → S → B`. Given an `S`-algebra homomorphism `f : A →ₐ[S] B`, the
`n`-division polynomial `ψₙ` of the base change of `W` to `B` equals the
coefficient-wise image, under the induced ring map `mapRingHom f` on the
bivariate polynomial ring `B[X][Y]`, of the `ψₙ` of the base change of `W` to
`A`. In words: forming the `n`-division polynomial commutes with base change.

Here `ψ : ℤ → R[X][Y]` is defined (project line 324, mathlib `Basic.lean:401`) as
`normEDS W.ψ₂ (C W.Ψ₃) (C W.preΨ₄)` — the normalised elliptic divisibility
sequence generated by the 2-, 3- and (pre-)4-division data. `mapRingHom f` is the
coefficient map `A[X][Y] →+* B[X][Y]` induced by `f`.

Variables / typeclasses (Lean side):
- `{R S} [CommRing R] [CommRing S]`, `W : WeierstrassCurve R` — the base curve.
- `[Algebra R S]`, `{A} [CommRing A] [Algebra R A] [Algebra S A] [IsScalarTower R S A]` — first target algebra.
- `{B} [CommRing B] [Algebra R B] [Algebra S B] [IsScalarTower R S B]` — second target algebra.
- `(f : A →ₐ[S] B)` — the algebra map along which we transport.
- `(n : ℤ)` — the division index.

Hypotheses (Lean side): none beyond the typeclass context.

Conclusion (math): `ψₙ` is natural in the base ring (commutes with base change).

Conclusion (Lean): `(W.baseChange B).ψ n = ((W.baseChange A).ψ n).map (mapRingHom f)`.

Proof body (2 rewrites): `rw [← map_ψ, map_baseChange]`. (Reduces base-change
compatibility to the bare ring-map naturality `map_ψ` plus the structural
`map_baseChange`.)

### Literature summary (Phase 3)

Concept identified as: the **bivariate `n`-division polynomials** `ψₙ` of a
Weierstrass curve (here realised as the normalised EDS `normEDS ψ₂ (C Ψ₃) (C
preΨ₄)`), and their **compatibility with base change** (functoriality in the base
ring).
Sources agree on the standard form: **yes** — the `Ψ_n` live in
`ℤ[a₁,a₂,a₃,a₄,a₆,x,y]` and are produced by a fixed recurrence with integer
structure constants, so applying any ring homomorphism to the (coefficients of
the) Weierstrass data commutes with forming them. This is folklore (Silverman III
exercises; standard in point-counting / Schoof references), not a quotable named
theorem.
Most general standard form: for **any** ring homomorphism of the base, `ψ_n`
commutes with coefficient-mapping. The project/mathlib lemma is stated for
`S`-algebra maps `f : A →ₐ[S] B` over a scalar tower — i.e. the relative
base-change formulation — which is one notch *more structured* than the bare
`R →+* S` form, but the bare-ring-map form is exactly the sibling `map_ψ`,
already present and `@[simp]`. The two together cover the full generality.
Generality dimensions: (i) base map = `R →+* S` (the `map_ψ` form) vs. `S`-algebra
map over a tower (the `baseChange_ψ` form); mathlib provides **both**. (ii) index
`n : ℤ` — fully general (negative indices via `ψ_neg`). No dimension where the
project form is missing.
Disagreement with the literature: **none**.
