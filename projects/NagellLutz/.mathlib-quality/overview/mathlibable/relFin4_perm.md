### Statement (Phase 1)

`EllSequence.relFin4_perm` states: let `R` be a commutative ring and
`W : ℤ → R` an **odd** sequence (`neg : ∀ k, W (-k) = -W k`). Define the
*four-index elliptic relation as a function of a tuple*

  `relFin4 W t  :=  rel₄ W (t 0) (t 1) (t 2) (t 3)`,  for `t : Fin 4 → ℤ`,

where

  `rel₄ W a b c d  :=  A a b · A c d  −  A a c · A b d  +  A a d · A b c`,
  `A m n          :=  addMulSub W m n  =  W ((m+n).tdiv 2) · W ((m−n).tdiv 2)`.

Then for every permutation `σ : Perm (Fin 4)` and every tuple `t`,

  `relFin4 W (t ∘ σ)  =  Perm.sign σ • relFin4 W t`.

In words: **`rel₄` is alternating (skew-symmetric) in its four arguments** — it
is invariant up to the sign of the permutation that reorders the indices.

Variables / typeclasses (Lean side):
- `{R : Type u} [CommRing R]` — the coefficient ring.
- `(W : ℤ → R)` — the integer-indexed sequence.
- `(σ : Perm (Fin 4))` — the permutation of the four indices.

Hypotheses (Lean side):
- `neg : ∀ k, W (-k) = -W k` — `W` is an odd function. (Used essentially: the
  per-swap sign flip `rel₄_swap₀₁/₁₂/₂₃` each invoke `addMulSub_swap`, which needs `neg`.)

Conclusion (math): `rel₄` is an alternating function of its four (integer) indices.
Conclusion (Lean): `∀ t, relFin4 W (t ∘ σ) = Perm.sign σ • relFin4 W t`.

Proof shape (≈ 8 lines): `Perm.mclosure_swap_castSucc_succ 3` says `S₄` is the
submonoid generated by the three adjacent transpositions
`swap i.castSucc i.succ`; `Submonoid.closure_induction` then reduces the goal to
(a) the generators — each handled by one of `rel₄_swap₀₁`, `rel₄_swap₁₂`,
`rel₄_swap₂₃` together with `Perm.sign_swap` — and (b) closure under
multiplication, via `map_mul` for `sign` and `mul_smul`.

### Literature summary (Phase 3)

Concept identified as: **skew-symmetry / alternating-ness of a (non-multilinear)
function of `n` arguments**, established via the standard fact that the
symmetric group is generated by adjacent transpositions plus the sign
homomorphism. The object `rel₄` itself is the "four-index elliptic relation"
from the Ward / Stange elliptic-net theory.

Sources agree on the standard form: **yes** — the principle "antisymmetric under
adjacent transpositions ⇒ sign-equivariant under all of `Sₙ`" is textbook and
universally agreed; the term of art for the resulting maps is *skew-symmetric*.
But it is consistently treated as a **routine argument, not a named standalone
theorem**.

Most general standard form: *for any type `α`, any commutative monoid / group
`M` (or just a type with a `ℤˣ`-action), and any `f : (Fin n → α) → M`: if
`f (v ∘ swap i.castSucc i.succ) = - f v` for every adjacent transposition, then
`f (v ∘ σ) = Perm.sign σ • f v` for all `σ : Perm (Fin n)`.*  (`rel₄`'s specific
algebraic shape is irrelevant to this extension step — only the per-swap sign
flip is used.)

Generality dimensions where the literature varies:
  - **what carries the sign**: a vector space / module (classical), or merely a
    type with a `ℤˣ`- (equivalently sign-) action (most general). Mathlib's
    existing version (`AlternatingMap.map_perm`) sits at the *multilinear-map*
    end and is therefore **too narrow** to cover `rel₄`.
  - **index type**: `Fin n` (concrete, as here) vs. an arbitrary `Fintype`
    with `DecidableEq`.

Disagreement with the literature: none. The user's statement is a correct,
specialized instance of the folklore principle.
