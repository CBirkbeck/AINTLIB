### Statement (Phase 1)

`EllSequence.HaveSameParity₄.perm` states: the 4-ary predicate `HaveSameParity₄` is invariant under every permutation of its four arguments. Concretely, for any `σ : Equiv.Perm (Fin 4)` and any tuple `t : Fin 4 → ℤ`, if `t 0, t 1, t 2, t 3` all have the same parity (equal `Int.negOnePow`), then `t (σ 0), t (σ 1), t (σ 2), t (σ 3)` also all have the same parity.

`HaveSameParity₄ a b c d` is *defined* (line 210) as the chain
`a.negOnePow = b.negOnePow ∧ b.negOnePow = c.negOnePow ∧ c.negOnePow = d.negOnePow`
— i.e. "all four integers are congruent mod 2". Mathematically this is a fully symmetric predicate (equality of all four parities); the Lean encoding as a chain of *adjacent* equalities is not syntactically symmetric, which is the only reason a `perm` lemma is needed.

Variables / typeclasses (Lean side):
- `σ : Equiv.Perm (Fin 4)` — an arbitrary permutation of the four index positions.
- `t : Fin 4 → ℤ` — the integer tuple (here always the index tuple of the EDS relation).
- (no ring/typeclass parameters — `W`, `R` from the section are not used; it is purely about `ℤ` parities.)

Hypotheses (Lean side):
- `HaveSameParity₄ (t 0) (t 1) (t 2) (t 3)` — the four entries share a parity.

Conclusion (math): the same-parity property is permutation-invariant (it is a symmetric predicate).
Conclusion (Lean): `HaveSameParity₄ (t (σ 0)) (t (σ 1)) (t (σ 2)) (t (σ 3))`.

Proof: `(Perm.mclosure_swap_castSucc_succ 3) ▸ Submonoid.mem_top σ` writes σ in the submonoid generated by the three adjacent transpositions, then `Submonoid.closure_induction` reduces to: identity (trivial), composition (compose the two tuple-relabellings), and each adjacent swap (`fin_cases i`, then reshuffle the three equalities). ~7 lines.

### Literature summary (Phase 3)

Concept identified as: "a fully-symmetric n-ary predicate (here: all four integers share a parity) is invariant under every permutation of its arguments" — the n=4, predicate=`same negOnePow` instance.
Sources agree on the standard form: yes — the underlying principle (symmetric group generated by adjacent transpositions ⇒ induction over swaps) is uniform across all channels and is exactly the proof used.
Most general standard form: for any type `α`, any `n`, and any predicate `P : (Fin n → α) → Prop` that is invariant under each adjacent transposition (equivalently, a `Symmetric`/fully-symmetric predicate), `P (t ∘ σ) ↔ P t` for all `σ : Perm (Fin n)`.
Generality dimensions where the literature varies:
  - arity: literature states it for arbitrary `n`; the Lean lemma fixes `n = 4`.
  - predicate: literature states it for an *arbitrary* symmetric predicate; the Lean lemma fixes `HaveSameParity₄` (one specific predicate).
  - carrier: literature is over any `α`; the Lean lemma fixes `α = ℤ`.
Disagreement with the literature: none. The Lean form is a triple specialisation (fixed arity, fixed predicate, fixed carrier) of a folklore principle that the literature never isolates as a named lemma.

## Verdict: `EllSequence.HaveSameParity₄.perm`

**Category:** NO-composable-from-mathlib

**Evidence:**
- Literature search (Phase 3): the underlying principle (symmetric group = ⟨adjacent transpositions⟩ ⇒ induct over swaps) is folklore and is exactly the proof; no source isolates "same-parity is permutation-invariant" as a named result. `HaveSameParity₄.perm` is a triple specialisation (fixed predicate/arity/carrier).
- Generality analysis (Phase 4): STRICTLY NARROWER THAN STANDARD — but the general form is itself only a thin wrapper over mathlib primitives (Phase 4c), so generalising does not earn a mathlib slot either.
- Mathlib search (Phase 5): not in mathlib (current or general form); the upstream `Mathlib.NumberTheory.EllipticDivisibilitySequence` has none of this machinery. Building blocks present: `Equiv.Perm.mclosure_swap_castSucc_succ`, `Submonoid.closure_induction`, `Equiv.Perm.swap_induction_on`.
- Composition check (Phase 6): COMPOSABLE — ≤3 mathlib calls + a trivial adjacent-swap parity reshuffle.

**Rationale:**

`HaveSameParity₄.perm` carries no mathematical content beyond mathlib's existing permutation-induction primitives. Its entire proof is `Equiv.Perm.mclosure_swap_castSucc_succ 3` (σ lies in the submonoid generated by the three adjacent transpositions) fed to `Submonoid.closure_induction`, where the identity and composition cases are formal and each swap case is a one-line reassociation of the three `negOnePow` equalities (`⟨h₀₁.symm, h₀₁ ▸ h₁₂, h₂₃⟩`). That is at most three mathlib calls plus trivial parity bookkeeping — the textbook definition of NO-composable. Decisively, the **same file already re-derives this exact pattern inline** for a different predicate in `relFin4_perm` (line 533: `mclosure_swap_castSucc_succ 3` + `closure_induction` for `rel₄` itself, no shared helper), and `perm` is even duplicated verbatim into the project's General/PID-track copy (`EllipticDivisibilitySequenceOriginal.lean:232`) — both signals of project-local glue, not a library-shaped lemma.

The need for the lemma at all is an artifact of encoding `HaveSameParity₄` as a chain of *adjacent* equalities (`a~b ∧ b~c ∧ c~d`) rather than a manifestly symmetric form. Mathematically "all four parities equal" is symmetric by inspection. Even that hypothetical generic lemma would be a ≤3-line wrapper over the same primitives, so it too sits below mathlib's granularity; the *specific* `perm` is firmly a NO. Cost is not a factor (the proof is cheap); this is purely "mathlib already gives you this in three calls."
